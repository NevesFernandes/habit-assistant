How Habit Assistant works

Instead of menus and forms, you tell the assistant what you want in plain words, typed or spoken. It does it, and only asks a question when it really can't act without one.

1. Talking to the assistant

Use the chat tab. Some things you can say:

To…Say something like
Add a habit add a habit to read every night, drink 8 glasses of water every day, meditate 10 minutes on weekdays
Add a recurring task add a recurring task to take out the trash every Monday and Thursday
Add a one-off task add a task to call the dentist tomorrow, add a task for Friday called Go shopping, with a checklist inside
Mark something done mark gym done, I went for a run yesterday, I watered the plants
Undo it actually I didn't go to the gym
Log an amount I drank 6 glasses of water, add 10 minutes to my reading today, log 30 minutes of meditation for yesterday
Use a list add milk and bread to my shopping list, check off milk, remove bread from my shopping list
Order your day move reading to the evening, put flossing just before bed, gym is a top priority
Change something move gym to Mondays and Thursdays, rename reading to evening reading, change my water goal to 10 glasses, remove the end date from gym
Take a break pause gym for two weeks, resume gym
Skip one day I'm sick, skip gym today, skip the bins on Thursday, unskip gym
Stop or remove archive the guitar habit, delete the dentist task
Add a note to a day mark run done, 5 km, legs tired, add a note to yesterday's gym: knee hurt, note for tomorrow's run: bring a towel, remove the note from today's run
Look back or ahead how did my week go?, what did I miss yesterday?, when did I last go to the dentist?, what's on next week?

Looking back changes nothing. For each item you get one line: how many of its days were done, which were missed, paused days (which don't count as missed), and what's still to do. A habit set to a number of times a week or month shows each week or month instead, since no single day is missed. Ask about one item and you also get the last day it was done, even if that was before the period. Up to a year at a time. Notes show under their item. When you ask about everything, a short summary comes first: how much of what was due got done, compared with the same number of days before, the longest current streak, anything done on every due day, the most improved item and the most missed one.

Notes on a day. Each habit or task can have one short note per day, like a journal line. It shows in italics under the item on that day in the Today tab. A new note for the same day replaces the old one. Un-ticking a day keeps its note, a missed day can have one, and so can a day still to come. Say the day (today's run, yesterday, on Monday): "notes" with no day means the item's description, which shows every day. A one-off task's note goes on the day you mark it. Deleting an item deletes its notes too.

When it needs more from you

  • If something is missing (say, how many glasses a day), the assistant asks. Just answer in the chat.
  • If a name matches several items, it lists them with numbers. Reply with the number, or say which one (the one in Health). To change several at once, give several numbers (1 and 3) or say both or all.
  • After adding something, it shows what was saved, including anything it assumed for you (like every day or starting today). For a schedule like every Monday, it names the first day it's due. If it got something wrong, tell it: no, only on weekdays.
  • Deleting always asks for a yes first. So does adding something with a name you already use.

Speaking instead of typing

Press and hold the microphone button, speak, and let go. What you said is turned into text and sent straight away, with no review step, and shows up in the chat like a typed message. A very short tap or a recording with no speech in it is ignored. Replies can also be read aloud: turn on Spoken responses in Settings.

2. Habits, recurring tasks and tasks

  • Habit: something you want to build and track over time, like reading, exercise or drinking water. Habits get streaks and stats.
  • Recurring task: a chore that repeats, like taking out the trash. It's just done or not done each time, with no stats.
  • Task: a one-off to-do, like calling the dentist.

Every item has a name and a part of the day, and can have a description, a start date (today unless you say otherwise) and an end date. Habits always have a category: if you don't name one, the assistant picks the best fit, or Other. Tasks can have a category or none: name one when adding the task (add a task to pay rent, in Finance) and it's saved with it.

To take something off an item without deleting it, say which part: remove the notes from the dentist task, remove the end date from gym, remove the category from the rent task. "Notes", "comments" and "details" all mean the description. A habit always keeps a category, so removing it moves the habit to Other. The same goes for a checklist: see Checklists on tasks.

Parts of the day

Every habit and task belongs to one part of the day, and the Today tab lists them in that order, so it reads from top to bottom like your day:

PartFor example, say
Wake-upfirst thing, when I get up
Morningin the morning
Work dayat work, during the day
Afternoonafter lunch, in the afternoon
Eveningafter work, tonight
Bedtimebefore bed

Say when something belongs as you add it: add a habit to floss every night before bed. If you don't, the assistant picks the part that fits best and tells you what it assumed, so you can correct it. To move something later, say move reading to the evening, or tap the ⋮ button at the end of its row in the Today tab and pick one of the six parts. The same buttons are on the item's own page in the View tab.

Inside a part, items are listed by name. To put one first, mark it important: gym is a top priority, or the Mark as important button under ⋮ or on the item's page. Important items get a star wherever they are listed, and come first in their part. To undo it: gym isn't a priority any more. There are no priority numbers.

Items you created before parts of the day existed show under Not placed yet at the bottom of the Today tab until you place them. Something you do twice a day, like brushing your teeth in the morning and before bed, is two habits, one in each part.

A habit is Yes/No unless you describe an amount, a duration or a list of steps. Number and Timer habits always need a goal; if you don't give one, the assistant asks.

Checklists on tasks

Any task can carry a checklist, like a shopping list. In the Today tab, a task with a checklist shows a small 3/7 badge: tap it to open the list, tick items and add new ones. When you tick the last item, it offers to mark the task itself done.

On a recurring task, each occurrence has its own checklist, in one of two ways:

ModeWhat happens next timeGood for
Carry over (the default) Ticked items stay behind on the day you ticked them. Unticked ones move on to the next occurrence, along with anything you add in between. add a weekly shopping task on Saturdays with a checklist
Same list The same items every time, all unticked again. An item you add joins the list from then on. add a cleaning routine every Sunday, same checklist every week: floors, windows

Switch any time: make the cleaning checklist reset every time or let unticked items on my shopping list carry over. To tick an item from an earlier day, say which day: I bought milk yesterday. In the Recurring tasks page, the checklist shown is today's.

Taking things off a list. Ticking an item says it got done; removing it says it shouldn't be on the list. Tap the small × next to an item, or say remove milk from my shopping list or take eggs and bread off the shopping list. The task itself is never deleted. On a recurring task the item is gone from that day on, and earlier days keep it as it was. The same sentence takes a step off a Checklist habit (remove floss from my morning routine); a Checklist habit always keeps at least one step.

Changing a Checklist habit's steps. Give the whole new list: change the steps of my morning routine to stretch, meditate and journal. Like a removal, the change counts from today: every earlier day keeps the steps it had, whether you ticked them or not. To make a change count from an earlier day, say so: remove floss from my morning routine since Monday. It can't start on a future day.

To take the whole checklist off a task, say remove the checklist from the shopping task. If the list still has items, the assistant shows them and asks first, because they are deleted with it. The task stays, as a plain task with no badge. Adding an item later starts a new list.

3. Schedules

Habits and recurring tasks repeat on a schedule. If you don't say one, it's every day.

ScheduleExample
Every dayread every day
Certain weekdaysgym on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, on weekends
Every few dayswater the plants every 3 days
A number of times per week or month, any daysrun 3 times a week, call grandma twice a month
A weekday of the monthpay rent on the first Monday of the month, on the last Friday
Dates every yearMum's birthday on March 15
On and off days5 days on, 2 days off

Change a schedule the same way: change reading to weekdays only. Weeks start on Sunday, or on Monday if you choose that in Settings (First day of the week). This decides what counts as “this week” for a times per week schedule. Changing it also regroups past weeks, so a past week may now count as met or missed differently.

4. Skip, pause, archive or delete

What happensHistory
Skip Excuses one day, today or later (I'm sick, skip gym today). It counts as neither done nor missed, so your streak survives. The Today tab still shows it, greyed out and marked Skipped, with an Undo. Past days can't be skipped, nor a day already done (undo it first). A habit set to a number of times a week or month has no set day, so there's nothing to skip. Kept
Pause Stops it for a while, with or without an end date. Paused days count as neither done nor missed, so your streak survives. A pause can't start in the past. Kept
Archive Stops it for good from today. It stays in your lists, marked Archived. Kept
Delete Removes it completely. The assistant asks first. Erased

Skip, pause and archive are for habits and recurring tasks; a one-off task can only be moved or deleted.

5. Rules that can surprise you

  • You can't complete a day that hasn't happened yet. Habits and recurring tasks refuse it. For a one-off task set in the future, you're asked whether to mark it done and move it to today.
  • Days follow your device's clock. A new day starts at midnight where your phone or computer is. If you travel, the app uses the new time zone the next time you open it.
  • You can only mark days an item is due. If gym is Mondays only, the assistant won't mark it done on a Tuesday, and tells you why.
  • Unfinished tasks carry over. By default, a one-off task that isn't done moves on to the next day, and keeps moving until you do it. If you want a task that simply disappears when its day ends, say so when you add it: add a task to call Ana, just for today.
  • Un-marking erases. Undoing a day removes what was logged for it, including an amount.
  • The timer is per device. If you close the app while it runs, it reopens paused, not with the missed time added. Save the result to a habit when you're done; nothing is logged until you do.

6. Around the app

Before you sign in, the app opens on its welcome page: Sign in with Google at the top, then a short introduction, a few things you can say, and links to this page, the privacy policy and a contact address.

The row of buttons under the title, from left to right:

  • Chat (speech bubble): talk to the assistant.
  • Today (calendar): what's due on a day, grouped by part of the day, with checkboxes. Pick another day from the strip of days (or its arrows); Today jumps back.
  • Categories (tag): add, rename or remove categories, and see stats per category. Every habit has a category (Other if nothing fits); tasks don't need one.
  • View (list): all habits, recurring tasks and tasks. Tap a habit for its details, stats and history, or any item to change its part of the day.
  • Stats (bar chart): overall progress, totals, a breakdown by category, and a heatmap you can switch between habits.
  • Timer (stopwatch): a stopwatch or countdown you can save to a Timer habit.

Next to the title, the ? button opens this page (also linked from the sign-in screen) and the ⚙ button opens Settings: your own AI key, voice options, spoken replies, and the first day of the week.

7. Stats and the heatmap

  • Current streak: how many due days in a row you've completed, up to today. Today doesn't break it until the day is over.
  • Best streak: the longest streak so far.
  • Completion rate: the share of due days you completed.

For a times per week or month habit, a period counts once you reach its number, the streak counts completions across periods that hit it, and the completion rate is the share of periods that did. A Number, Timer or Checklist habit counts as done for a day only once its goal is reached or all its steps are ticked.

Recurring tasks get the same numbers and heatmap as a yes/no habit, on their own page and in the heatmap picker of the stats tab. One-off tasks are counted together: how many were done this week, month, year and all time, how many are open today, and how many have carried over (and for how many days on average). Tasks done before this was added count in "all time" only. The stats tab shows habits, recurring tasks and one-off tasks in separate sections, so chores never change your habit numbers.

Starting the stats over

Say reset my gym stats, reset the stats of my Health habits or reset all my stats. The assistant asks first. Nothing is erased: the history and the heatmap keep every day, and only the counting starts again from today (today included). Streaks, completion % and every count restart; the best streak stays unless you say so (reset my gym stats including the best streak). Each page and the stats tab show Counting since: the start date, or the day of the last reset. Changed your mind? Say undo the reset. A single one-off task has no stats of its own; one-off tasks are reset together (reset my one-off task stats).

You can also ask in the chat: what's my gym streak?, how am I doing?, stats for my Health habits, how many tasks did I finish this month?

Reading the heatmap

One square per day, newest on the right, with the weekday letters on the side. Each column is one week, starting on your first day of the week.

  • Not due (not scheduled, or paused)
  • Missed
  • Partly done (a bit, about half, most)
  • Done
  • Today, not done yet

8. Your data and the AI provider

  • Your data is in your own Google Drive, in a folder called Habit Assistant, as one file, habit-assistant-data.json. You can open it, download it or back it up whenever you like. The app can only see files it created itself, not the rest of your Drive.
  • Several devices: sign in with the same Google account and they share the same data.
  • Staying signed in: Google's permission to reach your file lasts an hour at a time. When it runs out, a Reconnect banner appears. Tapping it opens a small Google window that closes by itself. If you changed something in the meantime, it waits and is saved as soon as you reconnect.
  • Chat messages go to an AI provider to be understood. By default that's the free shared trial (Google Gemini). The trial covers your first 50 messages; after that, the chat asks you to add your own key. Free tiers can use messages to improve the provider's models.
  • Voice recordings go to Groq to be turned into text, then are thrown away. They are never stored.
  • Your own key: if the shared trial is busy, unavailable or runs out (the chat will point you here), you can plug in your own free Groq or Gemini key (or an Anthropic key) in Settings. If a provider refuses your key (mistyped, or deleted on their side), the chat says the key was rejected: check it in Settings. A key is saved in your Drive file, in plain text, so every device you sign in on can use it, unless you tick Keep this key on this device only in Settings. Then it stays in this browser, and your other devices need their own key. A new Anthropic key starts with the box ticked, because it always has billing. What that means. How to get a free key.

The full details are in the privacy policy and the terms of service.

9. Using it from Claude

You can also add Habit Assistant to Claude as a connector. Claude then works on the same habits and tasks as the app, in the same Google Drive file: ask it what's left today, mark things done, add or change items, pause a habit, or look at your stats. Anything you change in Claude shows up in the app the next time you open it, and the other way round.

Adding the connector

  1. In Claude, tap your profile button to open the settings menu, then Connectors.
  2. Tap + and choose Add custom connector.
  3. Give it a name (for example Habit Assistant) and paste this URL: https://mcp.habitassistant.app/mcp
  4. Tap Continue, and sign in with the same Google account you use in the app. On Google's screen, keep the Google Drive box ticked: without it the connector has nothing to read.

On Claude's Free plan you can add one custom connector. If you already use another one, you'd need to remove it first, or be on a paid plan. On a Team or Enterprise plan, an owner of your organisation may have to add the connector before you can.

Asking before it acts

Tap the connector in Connectors to see its list of tools. For each one, Claude lets you choose Always allow, Needs approval or Blocked. With Needs approval, Claude stops in the chat and asks you to approve that one use, approve it always, or not approve it.

  • Reading your day, finding items, stats and everyday changes (marking done, adding, editing) are safe to Always allow; that's what keeps it quick.
  • Deleting items and resetting stats are up to you. Both already ask you in the chat before they change anything, so Needs approval on top is a second check.

Asking about "today"

Claude tends to read a plain what's on today? as a question for your calendar. Two ways round it:

  1. Recommended: a "Habits" project. Create a project in Claude (call it Habits), and paste the text below into its Custom instructions field, on the project's main page. Chat about your habits inside that project and plain questions just work.
  2. Without a project: name it in the question: what's on my habits today?, what's left in Habit Assistant?

This project is for my habits and to-dos in Habit Assistant. Questions about my day, what's on today, what's left, what I have to do or my routines are about Habit Assistant: use its tools. Don't ask for calendar access unless I mention the calendar or meetings.

If your calendar is connected to Claude too, you can ask for both at once: what's on today, habits and calendar?

Good to know

  • It follows the same rules as the app: the same parts of the day, no ticking future days, a pause can't start in the past, and deleting asks first.
  • Claude uses your device's time zone, which the app saves each time you open it; when travelling you can also just tell Claude where you are. If the app hasn't saved one yet, Claude asks you for your time zone once.
  • From Claude, resetting stats always keeps the best streak; to reset that too, ask in the app.
  • Claude gets the same numbers and summary the app works out, so you can ask it for a picture too: show my month in Habit Assistant as a calendar, chart my last four weeks of habits. Until then Claude says they aren't available yet (a completion with a note is still saved, without the note).
  • If Claude ever says it can't find one of Habit Assistant's tools, remove the connector and add it again (same steps as above), then start a new chat. Normally you never need to.
  • To stop using it, remove the connector in Claude's Connectors. What the connector stores, and how to remove it fully.
