Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Habit Assistant is a habit tracker you talk to. It's run by the Habit Assistant project (Vasco Fernandes, "we") at habitassistant.app. This page explains what happens to your information when you use it.

  • Your habits and tasks are stored in your own Google Drive, not on our servers.
  • The app can only see the files it created, not the rest of your Drive.
  • Chat messages go to an AI provider to be understood. Voice clips go to Groq to be turned into text, unless you switch on transcription on your device (section 4).
  • If you connect Habit Assistant to Claude, your items pass through our connector server on their way between Drive and Claude, which keeps your Google sign-in for it. See section 5.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. We don't sell or share your data.

1. Signing in with Google

You sign in with your Google account. The app asks Google for one permission, drive.file: to create and read files that this app itself created in your Google Drive. It can't see, change or delete any of your other files. The app doesn't ask for your name, email address or contacts. (Connecting Habit Assistant to Claude is a separate sign-in that does ask for your email address: see section 5.)

Google gives the app a temporary access token (valid for an hour) that it uses only to read and save your data file. The token stays in your browser.

2. Your habits and tasks

Everything you track (habits, tasks, checklists, categories, completion history, notes, goals and settings, including your device's time zone) is saved as one file, habit-assistant-data.json, in a visible Habit Assistant folder in your Google Drive. In the app it goes straight from your browser to Google Drive; it never passes through or is stored on our servers. The one exception is the Claude connector, if you choose to use it (section 5). You can open, download, back up or delete the file whenever you like. So that "undo that" works, the file also keeps a copy of what your last change by chat changed, from the app and from Claude; when that change was a delete, the deleted item and its history stay in the file until a change is made on a later day.

3. Chat messages and the AI provider

To understand what you type, the app sends it through our small server (a Cloudflare Worker) to an AI provider. Each message sends the current conversation (your messages and the app's replies, which can mention your items by name) and your list of categories. The assistant doesn't receive your whole data file. The conversation isn't saved anywhere: it's cleared when you close the app.

  • The free shared trial uses Google Gemini through our account, on its free tier. On that tier, Google may use what's sent to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. So don't type anything you wouldn't want seen. If the main provider is unavailable, a message may be answered by a backup provider instead (Cloudflare Workers AI or Groq).
  • Your own key: if you add your own Anthropic, Groq or Gemini key in Settings, your messages go to that provider under your own account and its terms. Your key is sent along with each message so our server can pass it on, and is never stored there. Keys are saved in your browser. For each key you choose whether it is also saved in plain text in your Drive data file, so your other devices can use it, or kept on this device only. See below for what that means.

What "plain text in your Drive" means for your keys

This applies to a key you let sync to your other devices. A key marked Keep this key on this device only in Settings is never written to the Drive file: see below.

A synced key is as safe as your Google Drive: Google protects the file, but Habit Assistant adds no encryption of its own. Anyone who can open your data file can read the key and use it. That includes:

  • anyone who gets into your Google account;
  • anyone you share the Habit Assistant folder or file with, even by accident;
  • other apps you've given access to your whole Drive (backup tools, file managers). Habit Assistant itself can only see its own files, but they can see everything;
  • copies of the file outside Drive, such as downloads, backups or a computer running Google Drive for desktop.

What a leaked key can cost depends on whether billing is attached to it, not on the provider. A free Groq key with no billing can only have its free allowance used up (and heavy misuse could get the account suspended). A Gemini key becomes a paying key if billing is ever turned on for its Google Cloud project. An Anthropic key is always paid, so a leak means someone else spending your credit.

To keep the risk small:

  • Keep a key with billing on this device only (Settings). A new Anthropic key starts that way.
  • Create a separate key just for Habit Assistant, so you can delete it without affecting anything else.
  • Set a monthly spending limit on any key with billing (the Anthropic Console and Google Cloud both offer one), so the worst case has a known ceiling.
  • Don't share the Habit Assistant folder, and delete the key at the provider if you think it has leaked.

A key kept on this device only

In Settings, each key has a Keep this key on this device only box. A new Anthropic key starts with it ticked, because an Anthropic key always has billing; Groq and Gemini keys start unticked. With the box ticked:

  • the key is saved only in this browser's local storage, never in your Drive file;
  • your other devices don't get it, and each needs its own key pasted in;
  • it is still sent with each message so our server can pass it on to the provider, and is never stored there;
  • it stays in this browser until you choose Forget this key or clear the site's data, also if someone signs in to Habit Assistant here with a different Google account.

If you tick the box for a key that was synced before, the key is removed from your Drive file. Google Drive keeps earlier versions of a file for about 30 days, and the key can still be read in those. Another device that still holds the old key can also write it back the next time you change a setting there, until you sign in again on that device. To be fully safe, create a new key at the provider, delete the old one, and save the new one with the box ticked.

Provider policies: Google Gemini API, Groq, Anthropic, Cloudflare.

4. Voice input

When you hold the microphone button, the recording is first checked on your device for speech. If there's speech, the clip is sent through our server to Groq's speech-to-text service, and the text comes back as your message. We don't store or log the audio.

Transcription on your device (a trial, off unless you switch it on in Settings): the clip is turned into text on your phone and never leaves it. To do that, the app downloads a speech model once from Hugging Face (Whisper, about 120 or 210 MB) and the software that runs it from jsDelivr (about 27 MB). Like any download, those services see your IP address; no audio or text is sent to them. The files stay in your browser's storage until you tap "Delete downloaded models". If transcription on the device fails, that clip goes to Groq as described above.

If you turn on "read replies aloud", your browser's built-in voice reads them. Depending on your browser and device, that voice may run online through your browser's maker (for example, Google for Chrome).

5. Using Habit Assistant from Claude

You can also add Habit Assistant to Claude (claude.ai) as a connector, so Claude can read and change your habits and tasks. This is optional and separate from the app. It runs on a second server of ours, mcp.habitassistant.app, also a Cloudflare Worker.

  • Signing in. When you connect, you sign in with Google again. The connector asks for drive.file (the same limited Drive access as the app) and your basic Google identity: a Google account ID and your email address, so it can tell your connection apart from other people's.
  • What we keep. So it keeps working after the first hour, the connector keeps the Google tokens for your connection (including a long-lived refresh token) in Cloudflare's storage, encrypted. Your Google account ID and email address are stored alongside them, not encrypted. If the connection isn't used for 90 days, all of it expires and is removed.
  • Your data file. Each time Claude uses the connector, our server reads your data file from Drive, does what was asked (for example, marks a habit done) and saves it back. Your items pass through the server only while that happens; they aren't stored there.
  • Claude. What the connector sends back (the items Claude asked about, and confirmations of changes) becomes part of your Claude conversation, and is handled by Anthropic under its privacy policy and your Claude settings, like anything else in that chat.
  • Logs. The connector logs which tool ran, whether it worked and how long it took. It never logs your items, names, notes or messages.

6. Our servers

Our app server only passes chat messages and voice clips on to the providers above. Neither it nor the connector server has a database of your habits, and neither keeps an account for you beyond the connector sign-in described in section 5. Cloudflare, which hosts it, keeps short-lived technical logs (such as request times, errors and IP addresses) for security and troubleshooting. We use these only to fix problems.

7. Stored on your device

The app keeps a few things in your browser's local storage: your AI key settings, a running timer, your read-aloud preference, and a random device ID (so each device can keep its own read-aloud setting). This is not used to track you. Clearing the site's data in your browser, or uninstalling the app, removes it.

8. Google user data

Habit Assistant's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from your Google Drive is used only to show and save your habits and tasks in the app and, if you connect it, to answer and carry out your requests in Claude. It isn't used for advertising, isn't sold, and isn't read by people, except when you ask us for help and share it yourself.

9. Deleting your data

  • Your data: delete the Habit Assistant folder in your Google Drive.
  • The app's access: remove Habit Assistant at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • This device: clear the site's data in your browser, or uninstall the app.
  • The Claude connector: remove it from your connectors in Claude, and remove its access at myaccount.google.com/connections (it may show up as a separate entry from the app). Once Google access is removed, the stored sign-in stops working within the hour, and it is removed after 90 days unused; email us if you want it removed sooner.

Since we don't hold your habit data, there's nothing else for us to delete.

10. Children

Habit Assistant isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their information.
